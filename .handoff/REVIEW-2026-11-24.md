## What it is [V on screen at Lisbon, 1728×1117, Metal]

- `js/wgsl/meadow-blades.js` (new), `js/meadow-grass.js` (rewritten past the cover map), `js/terrain.js`, `build.mjs`. No atlas: each frame five compute passes list the cells about the eye in ten nested levels (0.19 m to 96 m cells), sow up to 64 blades a cell from world-position hashes, keep a blade only while the blades about it lay no more than optical depth 5 seen from the eye (e^-5 of ground shows), lean it by the meadow wind field, and write it to one of three lists by its length on screen (6, 3 or 1 segments; three render pipelines, `drawIndirect`).
- A blade is a curved strip turned about its stem to face the eye. Past ~1 px wide it is widened (area kept) and coloured per pixel from the sward under it (`meadowBladeFieldTop`): the colour of the tip over that pixel, the wind's lean included, or the mean of many. Seen end on or far, it is a splat at its tip. One far-field rule at every range; no slab, no hand-off line.
- Species tables a coast (`MEADOW_SWARDS`: Portuguese hay meadow, hill pasture, margins; tropical); flowers in drifts; dry and seeding shares. Wind bands: gust field × billows carried downwind; laid blades lighten and take a sky sheen.
- Far blades never stand taller than they grow: past the levels' room they broaden, to 24 px. When the far list fills, short blades broaden next frame (word 15 of the draw buffer, written by the finish pass): overhead it settles at 80 % of room.
